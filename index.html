Three things a policymaker can mandate

Each recommendation is concrete enough to write into a procurement rule or an approval pathway.

01

Validate across ancestries before approval

Clinical AI and diagnostic tools should be required to show performance by ancestry group — not a single pooled metric — before they enter a health system.

Why: a pooled accuracy can look strong while the worst-served group is far below it. Polygenic scores lose roughly half their accuracy outside European ancestry.

02

Fund community-partnered sequencing

Sequence under-represented populations with community governance, Indigenous data sovereignty and benefit-sharing agreements, not extractive sample collection.

Why: representation without control reproduces the same power imbalance. Consent, custody and authorship are part of the dataset.

03

Mandate ancestry reporting in datasets and model cards

Every released dataset and model should publish its composition, known gaps and subgroup performance in a standard, machine-readable form.

Why: you cannot audit what is not reported. Composition disclosure turns an invisible bias into a procurement condition.
