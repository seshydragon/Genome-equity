Genomic equity · a data atlas

The human genome was read mostly from one part of humanity.

88.4% of the participants recorded in genome-wide association studies are of European ancestry. Africa — home to 18.6% of the world's people — accounts for 0.29% of that record.

The record is not a sample of humanity

A ratio of 1× would mean a region holds the same share of the genomic record as it does of world population. Nothing is close. Select a region to see its share of each.

Shares are quotients of published percentages, not per-capita participant counts. Ancestry is not residence: a European-ancestry participant in Texas counts toward Europe here.

A blind spot arrives as a test result

Four published measurements of what missing data does inside a clinic.

39.4%

The score was trained on someone else

A European-derived polygenic score retains 39.4% of its performance in African-ancestry patients, 46.6% in East Asian and 51.3% in South Asian patients. Martin et al. measured a 4.9-fold accuracy gap.

45.5%

More testing, less certainty

Among 493,515 people tested for hereditary cancer, 45.5% of Black patients received a variant of uncertain significance and nothing else — against 32.4% of non-Hispanic White patients.

25.3%

The test stops where the reference stops

The 23-variant CFTR panel finds both causal variants in 71.9% of white patients and 25.3% of Asian patients. A 329-variant panel lifts that to 86.0% and 53.9%.

191

The "rare" allele was never rare

Across 23 CPIC guidelines, 191 alleles were absent or unreported in the European reference group but present elsewhere. HLA-B*15:02 is carried by 6.9% of East Asians.

Method

This is an interactive reading of published data — no new cohort, no individual-level analysis. Derived figures are arithmetic on published values.
